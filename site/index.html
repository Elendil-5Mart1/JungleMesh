Message #samples

Open source

Every line of code is public, under the MIT license. Read it, audit it, build it yourself.

Peer to peer

Messages and files travel directly between devices, encrypted in transit. There is no JungleMesh server to leak, sell or shut down.

No data collection

No telemetry, no ads, no analytics. Your history lives on your devices, not in someone's cloud.

made for creatives

Built for people who make things

Most chat apps squeeze your photos, re-encode your audio and cap your files. JungleMesh hands them over exactly as you made them.

Lossless, untouched

WAV, FLAC, RAW, PSD, stems: files arrive byte for byte as you sent them. Nothing is recompressed, nothing is resized.

No size limit

A two-hour FLAC set or a zipped project folder goes through. Your friend downloads it straight from your device, piece by piece.

Listen in the chat

Samples and demos play right in the conversation (MP3, WAV, FLAC, OGG, M4A up to 25 MB). Nothing downloads before you press play.

Images at full resolution

Photos show up in the chat in their original quality, up to 25 MB; bigger ones arrive as files. You choose how long they stay: an hour, three days, forever.

Your links, without the tracking

SoundCloud, Mixcloud, Spotify and YouTube links turn into players that only load when you click. YouTube previews come from the sender, so the platform never learns who is looking.

your crew

Rooms, calls and screen sharing

  • Invite-only rooms with text and voice channels, for your label, your band or your crew.
  • Voice and video calls, up to 8 people in a voice channel, with noise suppression.
  • Screen sharing with sound (Windows), to show a mix, a timeline or a retouch live.
  • Markdown, reactions, edits and code blocks with syntax highlighting.

get started

Three steps, no sign-up

  1. Install and pick a name

    No email, no phone number, no password. Your identity is a key pair created on your device.

  2. Send your invite link

    Copy it from the app and send it any way you like: junglemesh://add/…

  3. Your friend accepts

    That's it: chat, share, call. Messages sent while they are away arrive when you are both online.

privacy

Private by design, honest about the limits

What you get

  • No account: no phone number, no email, nothing to link back to you.
  • Stealth mode: only your friends and room members can see you online.
  • Calls through a relay of your choice, to keep your IP address away from the people you call.
  • Encrypted backup of your identity, protected by a passphrase.
  • Update checks are the only time the app contacts something other than your peers, and you can turn them off.

What peer to peer means

  • Your contacts can see your IP address, as with any direct connection. A VPN hides it for chat.
  • You and a friend must be online at the same time at least once to connect.
  • A file waits on your device until your friend downloads it, so keep the app running (it lives in the tray).
  • Data is not encrypted on disk by the app: use your system's disk encryption.
  • Early beta, not audited by a third party yet.

faq

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No premium tier, no ads, no paid features. The code is open source under the MIT license.

Is it secure?

Connections are encrypted end to end (Noise for chat and files, DTLS-SRTP for calls), and there is no server that could read or keep your messages. It is still an early beta that nobody outside the project has audited: the security page lists what is protected and what is not.

Where are my messages and files stored?

On your devices and your friends' devices, nowhere else. Rooms are kept by their members: a message sent while someone is offline is passed on by the others.

Can I use it on my phone and my computer?

Yes, but an identity lives on one device at a time. The encrypted backup lets you move it from one device to another.

How do I report a bug or suggest something?

Open an issue on GitHub. For a security problem, follow the security page instead of posting it publicly.
